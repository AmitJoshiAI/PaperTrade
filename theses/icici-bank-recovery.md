## Why (the long form)

As of mid-October 2026 the Nifty has fallen for eight straight weeks, the longest run in 25 years,
to about 22,600. Foreign investors sold about USD 2.7 billion in September. The pressures named by
analysts are external: higher US yields, a firm dollar, rising crude and a weaker rupee. None of these
hurts the earnings of a large domestic bank directly; they hurt its share price through foreign
selling, because banks are the largest foreign holdings.

The macro reason: when external selling stops, the stocks that were sold for liquidity rather than
for fundamentals recover first. ICICI Bank is the cleanest large private bank by asset quality and
return on assets, is in every foreign portfolio, and reports Q2 FY27 results in late October, which
gives a near-term check on the "earnings are fine" half of the thesis.

Size Rs 1,00,000 (20% cap). Entry queued with decision_date 2026-10-16, filling at the first open
after that date. Close on 15 Oct 2026: Rs 1,389.40. Target Rs 1,600 (about +15%).
Hard stop Rs 1,320 (about 5% below the 15 Oct close), enforced by the engine.

## What would change my mind

- A domestic reason for the fall appears: a credit event, an RBI action on bank lending, or ICICI's
  own slippages rising. Then this is not "sold for liquidity" and the thesis is wrong.
- Foreign selling continues for another two months with the Nifty making new lows: the engine stop
  will have taken us out anyway.
